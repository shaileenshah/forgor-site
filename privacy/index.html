Privacy

Forgor holds dates. It is built so that the smallest possible amount of anything else passes through it.

What we keep

The dates you keep, and what you told us about them: a name, a kind, a day, whether it repeats, how far ahead you want the nudge, and any note you write. Your household, and who is in it. Your name, an email address and a phone number for your account, so that you can sign in with a code and your household can find you. A push token for each phone you use, so nudges and notices reach it, deleted when you sign out or delete your account. A date you mark for yourself only is visible only to you, not to the rest of your household.

Your contacts and your photos

When you choose to bring in birthdays from your contacts, your phone asks whether Forgor may access Contacts. If you allow it, Forgor reads only each contact's name, birthday and anniversary fields, on your phone, and shows you the list; you can untick anyone. Nothing is uploaded until you tap Keep and then agree, on a screen that explains it, to upload the people you ticked. For each person you keep, their name and that date are uploaded to your Forgor account on our server (hosted by Supabase) — that is how your list syncs across your devices, reaches the household members you share it with, and produces your reminders. Contacts you don't keep never leave your phone, and nothing else on a contact card (numbers, emails, addresses, photos, notes) is read. Names and dates from your contacts are never sold, used for advertising, or used to contact anyone; deleting the date, or your account, removes them from our server. When you scan a photo, the text is read on your phone and the image is never uploaded — only the date you choose to keep is saved. Text, links, mail or images you share to Forgor from another app are read on your phone; only the date you keep is saved.

Mail you forward

Each household gets its own forwarding address, shown in the app only after you have read how forwarded mail is handled and agreed to it. The subject, sender and text of mail sent there are sent to Anthropic, a third-party AI provider, whose Claude model finds the dates in it. Anthropic processes it only to return those dates to Forgor and does not use it to train its models. The mail itself is not stored. With each date it finds, Forgor keeps the date, the mail's subject line, the sender's name, any amount or place the mail gives for it, and an excerpt of at most 140 characters, so you can see where the date came from. Mail from an address that is not on your household's list is dropped without being read; only the address it came from is kept, for 30 days, so a member of your household can see it in Settings and add it; mail whose sender can't be verified is dropped without keeping anything. A date found in mail that nobody keeps or dismisses is deleted 60 days after it was found, or sooner once its day has passed.

Your calendars

If you choose to find dates in your calendars, Forgor first explains what happens and then your phone asks whether Forgor may read your calendars. Forgor only looks at the calendars you switch on, and never at calendars from work accounts it can recognise. It reads them on your phone, one year ahead. Before anything is sent, Forgor asks you separately whether event details may go to Anthropic's AI, and you can say no. Events you declined, placeholders such as Busy or OOO, and things that repeat every day are skipped on the phone and never sent. For every other upcoming event, including meetings and invitations, the title, day, time, place and how often the event repeats are sent to our server, which asks Anthropic's Claude model to name it and sort it. Anthropic processes them only to return that answer and does not use them to train its models. Each event appears only to you, as a suggestion. Nothing is added to your list until you tap Keep, and Forgor never changes your calendar. To avoid sending the same event twice, our server keeps a one-way code for each event it has seen (not its title) and whether you kept or dismissed it. A suggestion you haven't answered is deleted 60 days after it was found, or sooner once a one-off event's day has passed; one you dismissed is deleted 60 days after it was found. Turning the feature off deletes the ones you haven't answered, and deleting your account removes them all. Calendar data is never sold, used for advertising, or used to train AI.

Google Calendar (if you connect it)

If you connect a Google account under Settings → Sources → Google Calendar, Forgor's server reads, with read-only access, the list of your Google calendars and the events in the ones you switch on, one year ahead, whenever you open Forgor or pull down to refresh. Nothing in your calendar is ever changed.

Events you declined, placeholders such as Busy or OOO, and things that repeat every day are skipped on our server. For every other upcoming event, including meetings and invitations, the title, day, time, place and how it repeats are sent to Anthropic's Claude AI, only to name it and sort it. Anthropic does not use them to train its models. What's found is shown only to you, under “From your calendar”, and nothing is added to your list until you tap Keep.

We keep your Gmail address, your calendars' names, an encrypted sign-in key, and a one-way code for each event we've already looked at (not its title), until you disconnect. Disconnecting — or deleting your account — withdraws Forgor's access at Google and deletes the key, your calendars' names, the codes and any suggestions you haven't answered. You can also remove Forgor's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Google user data is never sold, never used for advertising, never used to train AI models, and never read by people except with your permission, for security, or as the law requires. Forgor's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

What we do not do

There is no advertising in Forgor, and nothing about you is sold or shared for marketing. Your dates are not used to train anyone's model.

Who else touches the data

Forgor runs on a small number of providers: Clerk (sign-in), Supabase (the database that stores your account, household and dates), Resend (receiving forwarded mail), Anthropic (finding dates in forwarded mail and in the calendar events you choose to share, described above), Apple (delivering notifications on iPhone) and Google (Firebase Cloud Messaging, delivering notifications on Android, and Google Calendar, if you connect it). They process the data on Forgor's instruction and for no purpose of their own.

This website

forgor.app counts visits with GoatCounter, which sets no cookies and keeps nothing that identifies you: only which page was opened, the site that sent you, and a rough country. The app itself has no analytics.

Deleting it

You can delete any date at any time. To delete your account, go to Settings → Account → Delete account in the app: the dates you added, your membership of the household, your devices' push tokens, any connected Google account (Forgor's access at Google is withdrawn) and your account are removed, and if you were the only member the household goes with them. Prefer to ask us instead, or no longer have the app? See forgor.app/delete-account or write to hello@forgor.app.

Children

Forgor is not intended for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect anything from them.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the app will say so rather than quietly updating this page.
